16 August, 2026

to Shihaam,

Happy birthday, shim.

I have been sitting here for quite some time now trying to write you a letter and it is rather ridiculous how a person can have so much to say and yet find himself incapable of writing even a single sentence that feels right.

It is almost nine in the evening and I have already begun to dislike every word I have written so far. I write something and then erase it and then write it again in a different manner as though the arrangement of a few words could somehow make my thoughts less foolish than they are. I suppose I am afraid of writing the wrong thing even here where you are not present to hear me speak and cannot interrupt me with that familiar little remark of yours that would probably make me forget what I was trying to say in the first place.

I have never written a proper letter to anyone before and I confess that I do not know how one ought to begin such a thing. I have always imagined that people who wrote letters to those they loved possessed some extraordinary ability to arrange their thoughts beautifully. Perhaps they knew precisely what to say and when to say it. Perhaps they did not spend an unreasonable amount of time staring at a blank page wondering whether the next sentence would sound sincere or merely absurd.

But I have never been particularly good at understanding myself and it would be rather presumptuous of me to expect that I should suddenly become capable of explaining myself simply because I have picked up a pen.

So I shall begin as I am able and forgive me if my thoughts wander for they have a habit of doing so whenever I think of you.

Do you know what I find most peculiar about human beings Shihaam? It is that we spend so much of our lives fearing the loss of something that we sometimes forget to take proper care of it while it is still ours. I was so afraid of losing you that I made our friendship a place of constant uncertainty for myself. I would see you with other people and feel something unpleasant stirring within me and instead of asking myself why I felt that way I would begin to wonder what they possessed that I did not. Why you could be so comfortable with them. Why they could occupy a place in your life without having to think about it while I seemed to spend so much of my time worrying about mine.

I remember feeling jealous of your friends. I remember seeing Shrelly on your pinned posts and highlights and feeling that strange little ache which I was too embarrassed to admit even to myself. I wanted to be someone important to you. Someone whose absence you would notice. Someone you would remember without needing a reason to do so. And perhaps somewhere along the way I began to believe that if I could only become important enough to you then I would never have to be afraid of losing you.

What a foolish bargain the human heart tries to make with life.

We ask for certainty from people who are themselves uncertain. We ask them to reassure us of things that no promise can permanently settle and then when they grow tired of answering the same fears we wonder how they could possibly leave us when we have done nothing but care for them.

I say this now with a certain shame because I know that I have done precisely that. I was so occupied with the possibility of losing you that I failed to notice how my own behaviour might make our friendship difficult for you. I wanted so desperately to feel understood that I did not always stop to consider whether I was understanding you in return.

And that is perhaps the part I find most difficult to forgive myself for.

After speaking to your friend I began to think about all the things I might have failed to understand about you. All the moments when you were struggling with something I could not see. All the feelings you might have kept to yourself while I was busy trying to make sense of my own. I wondered whether I had ever really understood you at all or whether I had simply grown accustomed to the version of you that I knew and mistaken that familiarity for understanding.

It is a terrible thing to discover that one may have been close to someone and yet still have so much left to learn about them.

For a while I even wondered whether I had ever been the person who understood you best as I had perhaps foolishly imagined myself to be. I wondered whether I had only been looking at you through the narrow little window of my own expectations and calling it affection.

And yet there are moments when I remember the smallest things about you. Things that you may have forgotten saying. Things that perhaps no one else would think worth remembering. And I find myself wondering whether I did understand you in some ways after all. Perhaps I simply never learnt how to make you feel understood.

There is a difference between the two and I fear I discovered it far too late.

You see I do not think that knowing a person means knowing every secret they have ever kept or understanding every thought that passes through their mind. Perhaps no one is ever truly capable of that. Another person is an entire world and we are only ever permitted to see certain streets certain rooms and certain windows illuminated in the dark. The mistake is in believing that because we have been welcomed into a few of those rooms we have understood the whole house.

I suppose I made that mistake with you.

But I do not wish to turn this letter into an attempt to defend myself. I have already spent enough time explaining my behaviour to myself and I have come to understand that an explanation does not necessarily make a hurt any smaller. I may tell myself that I acted as I did because I was afraid because I cared because I did not know how to handle my own thoughts but none of those things can change how my actions may have made you feel.

There is something almost humiliating about recognising one's faults only after they have begun to cost one something precious and yet perhaps humiliation is not such a terrible thing if it teaches us to look at ourselves without the usual excuses.

I wish I had learnt sooner.

I wish I had understood that friendship was not a competition in which I had to prove myself more important than everyone else. I wish I had known that your affection for another person did not have to mean that there was less room for me. I wish I had learnt to trust the moments we shared instead of constantly questioning whether they would last.

But the past has a cruel indifference to regret. It does not become kinder merely because we have finally understood what we ought to have done and it does not return our mistakes to us in a form that allows us to correct them.

We are left with the memory of what happened and the uncomfortable knowledge that we must now live differently if we are ever to become better than we were.

And so I have been thinking about friendship. About what it means to be a friend when being one becomes difficult and whether the affection we claim to feel for another person has any value if we are unwilling to examine the parts of ourselves that cause them pain.

I once heard someone say Maine agar koshishein hi nahi ki toh main dost kaisa and for some reason those words have remained with me. Perhaps because they express something I have been trying to understand for a long time.

If I stop trying to become better then what sort of friend was I to begin with?

But there is another question that troubles me and I do not know whether I shall ever find a satisfactory answer to it. If a person has been hurt so deeply that they can no longer bring themselves to forgive us what are we to do with the affection that remains within us? What does one do with all the apologies that never reach the person for whom they were intended? What does one do with the desire to make things right when the very person one wishes to make things right with no longer wishes to listen?

Perhaps one must learn to accept that being sorry does not entitle us to forgiveness. Perhaps the person we have hurt has every right to walk away even if we have finally begun to understand our mistakes.

I do not like this truth but I think I must learn to live with it.

I want to apologise to you Shihaam not because I believe that an apology will bring you back but because I owe you the honesty that I perhaps failed to give you when our friendship was still whole. I am sorry for the times when my fears became your burden. For the moments when I made you feel that you had to reassure me simply to keep things peaceful. And for the times when I was so concerned with whether you would leave that I failed to appreciate the fact that you were still there.

I am sorry that I did not understand sooner.

And I am sorry that I cannot go back to the beginning and become the person I should have been then.

I would like to tell you that I have learnt everything there is to learn from this and that I shall never make the same mistakes again but I think it would be dishonest to promise such a thing. People do not change merely because they have written a beautiful letter or spent a few nights regretting their actions. They change slowly through the small difficult decisions they make when no one is watching and when there is no one left to impress.

I can only hope that I shall become someone who understands that lesson a little better with each passing day.

You know Shihaam there is something profoundly unjust about time. It allows us to live through beautiful things without informing us of their worth and then leaves us to discover that worth only after the moments have passed. We laugh with someone and think there will be another evening another conversation another opportunity to say the things we forgot to say and then one day we find ourselves writing letters to a person with whom we once spoke so easily.

And suddenly the ordinary becomes extraordinary.

A conversation that once seemed insignificant becomes something we would give almost anything to experience again. A familiar name becomes difficult to look at and the smallest recollections begin to occupy a space in our minds far greater than the moments themselves ever seemed to occupy in our lives.

I wonder if this is what it means to miss someone. Not merely to wish for their presence but to discover that the world has quietly changed its arrangement in their absence.

I find reminders of you in the most unexpected places and sometimes I am almost annoyed by how effortlessly a memory can return without asking whether I am prepared for it. I could be thinking about something entirely unrelated and suddenly remember a conversation or something you once said and for a moment I forget that things are different now.

Then I remember.

It is strange how the mind can understand a fact long before the heart learns what to do with it.

I do not know how long it will take me to become accustomed to this. Perhaps a month perhaps a year perhaps two years or perhaps the memory of you will simply become one of those things that no longer hurts but never entirely disappears. I cannot say and I do not wish to pretend that I know what the future holds.

But I do know that I shall always be grateful that I knew you.

Thank you for having been a part of my life. Thank you for the conversations that asked for nothing and the laughter that needed no occasion. Thank you for all those little moments that seemed ordinary when they happened and have become precious to me now that I can no longer return to them in the same way.

I do not think you realise how much a person can leave behind in another person's life without ever intending to do so. A phrase a habit a way of looking at something a memory that returns years later for no apparent reason. We pass through one another's lives leaving little pieces of ourselves behind and often we never discover where those pieces eventually settle.

Perhaps I have left a few pieces of myself in your memories too. Perhaps you shall remember something foolish I once said and laugh about it someday. Perhaps you shall remember me with irritation perhaps with indifference or perhaps not at all.

I cannot ask you to remember me fondly and I would never wish to turn even this letter into a demand upon your heart.

I only hope that whatever you remember of me you will know that our friendship was something I valued even when I did not know how to take proper care of it.

And I hope you are doing well. I hope you have people around you who make you feel comfortable enough to be yourself. People who listen when you speak and notice when you fall silent. People who understand when you need company and when you need to be left alone. I hope life gives you the sort of happiness that does not require you to explain yourself to anyone.

Even if I am no longer someone with whom you wish to share those things I still hope you find them.

Perhaps that is the most difficult part of caring about someone after things have gone wrong. That you must learn to wish them happiness without assuming you shall have a place in it.

I am still learning.

I do not know whether I shall ever get the opportunity to sit with you again and talk as we once did. I do not know whether you shall ever wish to hear my apology or whether this letter will remain something I wrote and never had the courage to give you. But if someday there comes a time when you feel that you can speak to me again I would be grateful for the opportunity to begin differently.

Not to pretend that nothing happened and not to demand that we become what we once were but simply to meet one another again as two people who have had time to think and perhaps have learnt something about themselves.

I would like to be your friend again if you ever wish for that too.

And if you do not I shall have to learn to respect that even if it takes me time to understand how.

For now I suppose all I can do is carry the memory of our friendship with me and try to become a better person because of it rather than remain forever trapped in the person I was when I had it.

I am happy that I got to know you Shihaam. I am happy that for a little while our lives crossed and that I had the chance to call you my friend. And I really do like you as a person more than I ever managed to express properly.

Perhaps I should have told you these things when there was no distance between us. Perhaps I should have been less concerned with whether I mattered to you and more concerned with showing you that you mattered to me.

But I am telling you now because I do not wish to let my mistakes become the only thing that remains unsaid between us.

I began this letter because I wanted to find the right words and I am ending it with the uncomfortable realisation that perhaps there are no right words for everything we feel. Sometimes we write and rewrite and search through books for the language of people who lived long before us hoping that someone else might have discovered a way to explain what we cannot.

And perhaps they did.

Perhaps every writer who ever sat alone with a page before him was trying to do the same thing. To make sense of the heart and its strange contradictions. To understand why we hurt the people we wish to keep close why we recognise the value of things only when they are no longer within our reach and why even after all our mistakes we continue to hope that something good might still come of them.

I do not know if this letter has managed to say what I wanted it to say but I suppose I shall have to let it remain imperfect because I am imperfect and these words are mine.

Take care of yourself Shihaam.

And if life should ever bring us to the same place again I hope that by then I shall have learnt to be a little less afraid a little more understanding and a much better friend than I knew how to be before.

Until then I shall remember you with affection and I shall be grateful for the time we had without pretending that gratitude makes the distance any easier.

Some people become memories before we are ready to let them go and some friendships leave us with lessons we spend years learning to understand.

You have been both a beautiful part of my life and a lesson I shall carry with me.

And whatever becomes of us I am glad that I knew you.

Yours sincerely and with affection

- Nikhil
